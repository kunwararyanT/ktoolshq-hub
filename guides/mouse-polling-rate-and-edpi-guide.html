Mouse Polling Rate (1000Hz vs 8000Hz) & eDPI Aim Matching

In competitive tactical shooters like CS2 and Valorant, mechanical consistency is everything. If your hardware is lagging or your sensitivity multipliers are inconsistent across different games, you are actively destroying your muscle memory.

Understanding Mouse Polling Rates (Hz)

Your mouse's polling rate dictates how many times per second it reports its position to your CPU. A standard gaming mouse runs at 1000Hz (reporting 1,000 times per second, or every 1 millisecond). Recently, peripheral manufacturers have pushed "hyper-polling" mice running at 4000Hz or 8000Hz (0.125ms latency).

While an 8000Hz polling rate technically reduces input lag, it comes with a severe cost: CPU thread overhead. If you do not have a top-tier CPU, forcing your system to process 8,000 mouse inputs every second can actually cause stuttering and frame drops in CPU-heavy games like Valorant.

What is eDPI and Why Does it Matter?

If you play at 800 DPI with a 1.5 in-game sensitivity in Counter-Strike, how do you get that exact same physical feeling in Apex Legends? You calculate eDPI (Effective Dots Per Inch). Because different game engines use completely different mathematical multipliers for raw mouse input, relying purely on in-game slider numbers is useless.

To build perfect muscle memory, you must measure your sensitivity by "cm/360"—the physical distance in centimeters your mouse must travel on your mousepad to perform a perfect 360-degree turn in-game. By using an eDPI converter, you can guarantee that a 180-degree flick shot requires the exact same muscle twitch regardless of what game you are launching.
